01 Why this exists

Two different skills

AI engineering

Building and researching AI systems yourself: models, training, infrastructure. Most existing resources serve this group.

AI in engineering

Using AI as a working tool inside the discipline you already practice, with the domain judgment to check what it gives you. That's what this is for.

The research

A Wharton study in Management Science (Tambe) found that firms get the most value from AI when algorithmic literacy is spread across domain experts rather than concentrated in a specialist team. AI output needs contextual, domain-level judgment to be trustworthy, and that judgment doesn't transfer easily between people. Read the paper

An engineer who's fluent in their discipline and in using AI well is a different, more valuable thing than either one alone.

02 The toolkit

A study system you can copy

Claude Project templates and worked examples, generalized from one engineering student's own AI-assisted academic system. Free, MIT-licensed, and meant to be adapted, not followed like a script.

  • Semester HQ

    One Claude Project that coordinates across all your courses: deadlines, load-balancing, the big picture.

  • Course projects

    A per-course template, filled in with your own syllabus, that plugs into Semester HQ.

  • My week

    A semi-static weekly schedule, updated only when the pattern itself changes.

  • Context log

    An append-only log of your situation, so you're not re-explaining yourself every session.

  • Workflow examples

    Real coursework: a simulation that won't converge, sanity-checking a hand calc, scaffolding a report, parsing a dense datasheet.

  • Know when it's wrong

    Worked examples of AI being confidently, plausibly wrong on engineering details, and how to catch it. The most important part.

Not an answer generator

This is an organizing and studying system. Nothing in it is designed to produce work you hand in as your own. If AI writes your lab report's analysis, you've defeated the point, which is making you faster and sharper, not replacing you.

Get started

  1. Copy the Semester HQ template into a new Claude Project.
  2. Make one Claude Project per course from the course template.
  3. Read “Know when it's wrong” before you rely on any of it.

03 Know when it's wrong

Right shape, wrong substance

AI tools are fluent, confident, and sometimes wrong in ways that sound completely plausible. You get the structure of a correct answer, the tone of confidence, even the right units, with the wrong value inside. Domain knowledge is what catches it.

Unit consistency

Shape ✓Substance ✗

A stress or load conversion lands in the wrong unit system while the explanation around it reads perfectly. It matched the shape of a conversion without checking the arithmetic.

Catch it Sanity-check the magnitude against physical intuition. Don't just trust that a conversion happened.

Material properties

Shape ✓Substance ✗

A yield strength or thermal conductivity that's the right order of magnitude but wrong for your specific alloy, temperature, or condition, because it's a general value rather than yours.

Catch it Check any property against an actual datasheet before it goes into a real calculation, especially anything safety-relevant.

The judgment has to come from somewhere, and it has to be yours.
