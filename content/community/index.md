The Engineers with AI community runs on three discussion boards on GitHub. Use them to share what you've built with AI, ask questions, and post engineering problems that AI might help solve. Anyone can read the boards; you need a GitHub account to post.

Read the [rules](#rules) before you post. The most important one: never post confidential, proprietary, client, personal, or export-controlled material.

## Show and tell {#show-and-tell}

Share things you built or did with AI in your engineering work: a script that automates a lab chore, a calculation you checked, a design review where AI helped, or an attempt that didn't work. Say how you checked the result. Posts that show what the AI got wrong, and how you caught it, are among the most useful.

Ask about the Starter Kit, AI tools, or using AI in your field. Search the board first, since someone may have asked already. Say what you're trying to do and what you've tried, and paste error messages as text.

## Problems to solve {#problems-to-solve}

Post real engineering problems that AI might help solve, for other members to discuss or take on as a project. Good sources are teaching labs, student design teams, research groups, and your own coursework. Describe the problem, how it's done now, what data exists, and how a result would be checked.

Post only what you're allowed to share publicly. A problem from an employer or a client usually isn't yours to post, even with details removed, unless they have agreed to it.

## Rules {#rules}

- **Never post confidential, proprietary, client, personal, or export-controlled material.** That includes data, drawings, code, and screenshots from an employer, a client, or a sponsored project, and anyone's personal information. If you're not sure whether something can be shared, don't post it, and ask your supervisor or instructor first.
- **Say how you checked AI-assisted results.** Name the checks you ran and what they showed.
- **Credit your sources.** Link to the papers, datasets, standards, and code you used, and say which parts of your work came from someone else.
- **Be respectful.** Criticize the work specifically and constructively. Personal attacks and harassment aren't allowed.
- **Moderators may remove posts** that break these rules.

## At UIUC {#at-uiuc}

An in-person community is starting at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), where Engineers with AI began. If you're at UIUC and want to take part, say so on the [Questions](#questions) board.

## Get involved

1. Create a GitHub account if you don't have one, and sign in.
2. Open the [community discussions on GitHub](community:discussions) and read the rules above.
3. Start with a post in [Show and tell](#show-and-tell) or [Questions](#questions).
